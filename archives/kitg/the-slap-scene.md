The audience can feel it coming. A woman whispers low, “He’s gonna get slapped.” Men cringe in anticipation, women gleam with expectation. All eyes are glued to the screen waiting to see how justice is served.

On screen she raises a menacing finger, and decides how she wants to play it. Should she slap him? The flat whack of her open hand across his face, the recoil of his head and the reddening finger-shaped welt spreading on his cheek? She feels like it’s letting him off the hook.

She balls her raised hand into a fist. Perhaps a hard right to his chin would deliver her message or at least break his jaw. But it’s almost too violent; too male.

In the end she decides a swift kick straight to his balls is the perfect choice. She takes one step back and drives the top of her foot up and under his balls lifting him off the ground. She stands back and watches him drop like a stone at her feet.

The audience gasps collectively. Men squirm in their seats and instinctively cover their own groins. Several women nod approvingly and one can’t keep herself from blurting, “Oooh, you go girl!” Excitement crackles in the air like electricity as they watch the man onscreen quiver and groan in tremendous pain inflicted on him by a woman. The tension between the sexes is heavy, men suddenly filled with uncertainty about the strength of their manhood, woman elated with the demonstration of girl power.

Onscreen there’s a subtle shift. The woman smiles down at the broken man but there’s no malice or lingering fury. She looks almost angelic and her eyes are soft with compassion.

The buzzing audience stills and takes in this unexpected turn.

The man looks up at the woman with an expression of gratefulness and there is an aura of reverence on his countenance. He reaches a hand to the woman and she takes his in return. She holds it gently as she watches him still struggling with the pain.

The audience is at a loss. How can this man reach out to the very woman who caused the aching fire in his balls? Why isn’t she mocking his pain?

Onscreen the woman leans toward the man and her whisper in his ear comes out like a roar in the silent theater. “Now you are a man.” Mustering the little strength he has, he nods his affirmation and accepts the relief that comes as he collapses into the fetal position.

The woman continues to stand over him, tall and proud, accepting her role of dominance.

Very few in the audience understand what they have witnessed. Men who understand have themselves already become a “man.” A woman has brought them into manhood with a humbling kick and they have recognized the mercy she has shown. Women who understand have themselves have crushed a man’s balls, relieving him of responsibility of ultimate authority.

Men burn with the need to control, to dominate and show strength, but the desire for power corrupts intentions. That, and the knowledge that because of their testicles, they can never fully reach the heights of power they aspire to, weighs so heavy on their psyche.

On the other hand, a woman, who can never experience the gut-wrenching, tear-welling, humiliating pain of busted balls, can truly reach the heights of power a man so craves. She has no ultimate weakness. There is no tension in her desire for power because she already has it – granted to her at birth. Her femininity is her power. And there is no better display of her femininity than to deliver searing pain by kicking a man in his balls. Such a kick instantly reveals her ultimate, uniquely feminine power. A smart man will recognize that in doing so, she lifts the burden of having to attain something he can never reach: domination, ultimate power and complete authority. He understands his place and fully accepts hers. Oh what mercy she shows in breaking his balls.
