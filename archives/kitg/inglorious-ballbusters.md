CHAPTER 1 - Somewhere in England

Lieutenant Anna Rainier walked slowly down the line of soldiers at crisp attention. Not a wrinkled uniform nor a hair out of place would be accepted. Lt. Anna had no time or patience for disorder of any kind among her soldiers - except of course for the disorder she was about to ask of each and everyone of them.

Lt. Anna was born in one of the southern states in the US, but no one knew for sure which one. She didn’t talk about that part of her life, nor did she talk about the lengthy scar that disfigured her otherwise perfect face. The scar began just above her left eye and ended just at the curve of her left jawline. But it was her striking blue eyes, fantastic figure, and air of confidence that drew the attention of anyone in her presence - especially men - so much so that no one dared ask about the scar.

She stopped in the middle of the line, ready to address the female soldiers that stood before her.

“My name is Lt. Anna Rainier,” she began. “You are all here because you have special skills that have set you apart from others in your field.  And I am looking for those special skills right now. I am putting together a team unlike any other team in this theater of operation. While I am sure each and every one of you want to win the war, that is not why you are here. You are here for one reason and one reason only….to kill Nazis. We will be going behind enemy lines, we will be dressed as civilians, we will infiltrate enemy strongholds, we will disrupt enemy supplies, and we will kill every Goddamned Nazi that has the misfortune of crossing our path.” Anna paused to assess the soldiers response. They all remained stoic and in place. “We will be cruel to the Germans in ways they cannot imagine. They will fear the night knowing that at any moment we could come for them, and they will whisper our names and relive our cruelty in their nightmares.” Anna paused once more. “Are you with me?”

“Yes, sir!” they responded in unison.

“Well all right then. That’s what I like to hear.” Anna walked down the line. “But be warned, when you join my command you take on a debt to me. One that I expect to be repaid. Every single soldier in my command personally owes me 100 Nazi ballsacks. And you will get me those balls, one way or another, so that every soldier in that god forsaken army knows what’s comin’ to them. Am I clear?

“Yes Sir!”

“Well, all right then.”

  
  

CHAPTER 2 - Somewhere in occupied France

The “Ballbusters” (as they will be referred to collectively) gathered around a group of dead Nazi soldiers at the edge of a single artillery battery. Four dead bodies were lined up alongside each other like cord wood. Each soldier had a single precision bullet wound to the forehead. Two of them also had bloody wounds in the groin area. Two live soldiers were being held prisoner at the edge of the gun wall.  Sitting on a bluff off to the side were the creators responsible for the carnage: Catherine and Elise Dubois.

Catherine and Elise DuBois, the “twins” as they are referred to by some, at 16 years old were training to be ballerinas at the Paris Opera Ballet school when tragedy befell their family. At the outset of the war, gestapo soldiers raided their farm, convinced that their father was harboring Jews. When no Jews were found, the soldiers slaughtered their mother, father, younger sister and brother out of spite. Upon learning of the tragedy, both girls enlisted in the French resistance and were trained as snipers. They took to it immediately and began racking up confirmed kills by the dozens. Now at 19 years old, their desire to extract payment from the German army was still insatiable. So much so that their preferred mode of kill was a well placed shot to the balls  - especially if the enemy was an officer - followed by a kill shot to the head.

From what the Ballbusters had gathered from other victims, word had already circulated among the Nazi high command that female snipers were targeting the testicles of field officers throughout the region. This pleased the twins to no end, and horrified the Nazi leadership such that officers were reluctant to take field commands. Much to their dismay, they had no choice in the matter.

“Bring that Nazi sergeant over here,” said Lt. Anna.

One of the Ballbusters (with fiery red hair, who we will meet later) dragged the Nazi soldier closer and kicked him in the ass so that he fell at the feet of Lt. Anna. While still on his knees, he straightened his back and stood at attention as best he could.

Lt. Anna smiled. “English?”

The German shook his head. “Nein.”

“That’s too bad,” said Anna and she planted a quick kick into his crotch, dropping the Nazi sergeant to the ground once more. The other Ballbusters cheered and applauded. “Pick him up.”  Two of the Ballbusters, one blond and one brunette, lifted the moaning soldier to his feet, holding his arms so he couldn't move.

“Ask him if there are other artillery batteries near here.”

The blond repeated the question in fluent German. The Sergeant stood tall and shook his head as if to say “I won’t answer.” Lt. Anna nodded to the brunette, who then stepped in front of the German and drove a hard knee into his testicles. The sargeant expelled a rush of air, but the Ballbusters held him up.

“Ask again,” said Lt. Anna.

The blond repeated the question. The German shook his head again. Without a nod, the blond stepped forward and drove another knee into his balls. The sargeant cried out and coughed. He was held up once more.

“Ask again,” said Lt. Anna.

The question was repeated once more in German. The Sergeant swallowed hard, and nervously looked back and forth at all the Ballbusters. With one last defiant effort, he shook his head again.

Lt. Anna drew her pistol and shot the German in the balls. He fell to the ground, bleeding and scratching in pain. She then faced the second German soldier who was being held at the side by another of the famed Ballbusters. As she stepped over the crying Sergeant she leveled a round into his head, killing him instantly.

The terrified corporal began crying out in German, pointing to some location in the distance.
